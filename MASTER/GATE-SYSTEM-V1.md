# D&D 5E Hidden-Object Gate System V1

> **Hard rule:** No downstream production starts before the required gate is explicitly approved.

## GATE 0 — Lore Lock
**Purpose:** prove the location before designing it.

**Inputs:** official sources, location research, evidence labels, maps/art references.

**Pass:** location identity, factions, resident/visitor logic and species logic are sourced; unknowns are marked; project inference is labeled.

**Fail return:** Stage 01 — Research.  
**Unlock:** subscene selection.  
**Human approval:** yes, packaged with Gate 1 where practical.

## GATE 1 — Scene Lock
**Purpose:** reduce a large location to one drawable moment.

**Inputs:** candidate scorecard, chosen subscene, scene state.

**Pass:** one subscene, one time slice, one main event; one-sentence summary; representative and distinct from other levels.

**Fail return:** Stages 02–03.  
**Unlock:** population modeling.  
**Human approval:** yes.

## GATE 2 — Population Lock
**Purpose:** establish who belongs in the scene and how groups look.

**Inputs:** population model, species scale, faction/cultural visual language.

**Pass:** local population forms the structural base; visitors are justified; unusual species are not added merely for variety; scale is stable; groups are visually coherent; crowd density is readable and plausible.

**Fail return:** Stages 04–06.  
**Unlock:** character asset production.  
**Human approval:** yes.

## GATE 3 — Asset Lock
**Purpose:** approve the actual cast, props and creatures before composition.

**Inputs:** character table, white-background character sheets, props, creatures.

**Pass:** required cast exists; species scale is stable; full bodies/faces are readable; faction unity without cloning; equipment matches role; style remains locked.

**Fail return:** Stages 05–08.  
**Unlock:** event islands and blocking.  
**Human approval:** yes.

## GATE 4 — Blocking Lock
**Purpose:** prove the ensemble composition before the final scene.

**Inputs:** 8–12 event islands, character placement, scale layers, background functional plan.

**Pass:** people are the subject; no deep-perspective collapse; primary events read; rear figures remain readable; density is distributed; location anchors survive; eye-lines/interactions are coherent.

**Fail return:** Stages 09–10.  
**Unlock:** final scene generation.  
**Human approval:** yes.

## GATE 5 — Scene Lock / Visual QC
**Purpose:** approve the scene as an illustration before game mechanics are added.

**Inputs:** integrated scene and correction pass.

**Pass:** proportions/species anatomy stable; expressions varied; faces/hands readable; equipment/action credible; location recognizable; magic purposeful; background subordinate; scene works without UI.

**Fail return:** Stage 12 or the smallest earlier stage causing the failure.  
**Unlock:** hidden-object design.  
**Human approval:** yes.

## GATE 6 — Hidden-Object Lock
**Purpose:** approve what is being searched for and why the hiding is fair.

**Inputs:** target set, IDs, hiding positions, clue concepts, difficulty distribution.

**Pass:** targets are varied and diegetic; fair but not obvious; not dependent on microscopic pixels; difficulty spread intentional; choices do not damage the scene.

**Fail return:** Stage 13 unless a deeper visual fault is exposed.  
**Unlock:** level-data/UI packaging.  
**Human approval:** yes.

## GATE 7 — Level Lock
**Purpose:** approve the complete playable handoff package.

**Inputs:** final scene, targets, answer map, clues, icons, UI, level metadata.

**Pass:** every required target has an answer location and required clue/icon; IDs agree across files; UI preserves composition; metadata identifies level/version; package is internally consistent and implementation-ready.

**Fail return:** Stage 14 unless a deeper hidden-object/scene fault is exposed.  
**Unlock:** archive / level complete.  
**Human approval:** yes.
