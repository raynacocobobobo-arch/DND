# D&D 5E Hidden-Object Production Standard V1

## 1. Product definition

The project delivers **15 complete Hidden-Object game levels**, not merely research files or illustrations. Every workflow stage exists to produce and maintain those playable level packages.

A level is complete only after **Gate 7 — Level Lock**.

## 2. Evidence classes

Use only:
- `【SRD-5.1】`
- `【SRD-5.2.1】`
- `【OFFICIAL-SETTING】`
- `【OFFICIAL-INDEX】`
- `【PROJECT】`

`【PROJECT】` must never be presented as official lore.

## 3. Shared vs per-level assets

### Shared
- master 5E/SRD research;
- global style lock;
- workflow and gate definitions;
- reusable templates.

### Per level
- location/subscene research;
- population ecology;
- species scale;
- faction/culture visual language;
- scene-specific casting set;
- props/creatures;
- event islands;
- actor blocking;
- final scene;
- targets/answers/clues/UI/metadata.

There is **no global reusable actor library**. Each scene casts its own population.

## 4. Crowd standard

Default design target: **approximately 55–70 readable figures**, normally around 60.

Scene logic outranks quota. A giant court, isolated settlement, or creature-heavy scene may justify a different balance if Gate 2 explicitly approves it.

A credible crowd is not 60 player characters. Build from:
1. permanent residents;
2. institutional staff;
3. temporary visitors;
4. special narrative roles;
5. non-humanoid creatures.

## 5. Species standard

Species consistency precedes individual variation.

Lock first:
- height range;
- skeleton/body mass;
- head/body proportion;
- ears/horns/tail/scales;
- silhouette.

Then vary:
- age;
- hair/beard;
- body type within the species range;
- clothing;
- profession;
- equipment;
- expression.

Do not distort species anatomy merely to make every character unique.

## 6. Faction/culture standard

Group identity should be visually coherent. For each important faction/culture define:
- materials;
- palette range;
- garment silhouette;
- armor tendency;
- insignia;
- typical tools;
- forbidden elements.

Individual variation happens inside this shared language.

## 7. Magic standard

Magic must perform a task. Examples:
- Mage Hand moves or retrieves something;
- Mending repairs;
- Detect Magic investigates;
- Identify identifies;
- Bless prepares people for danger;
- Minor Illusion communicates or demonstrates;
- Druidcraft reads or influences a small natural condition.

No decorative “everyone holds a glowing orb” treatment.

## 8. Event-island standard

A finished ensemble scene normally contains **8–12 event islands**.

Typical island size: 3–7 figures.

Each island should include:
- primary action;
- secondary action;
- eye-line relationship;
- reaction/conflict;
- at least one readable prop.

Priority:
- 3–4 A-level primary islands;
- 4–5 B-level secondary islands;
- 2–4 C-level detail/humor islands.

## 9. Composition standard

Core rule:

> **a character comic that contains a setting, not a big setting containing many characters**

Use weak/compressed perspective and a stage-like horizontal composition.

Recommended scale compression:
- foreground: 105–110%;
- core midground: 100%;
- rear/platform: 85–90%.

Do not shrink rear characters into unreadable points.

Characters are blocked first. Background is fitted around their actions.

## 10. Background standard

Priority:
1. location-recognition anchors;
2. structures required for actions;
3. circulation/path logic;
4. functional props;
5. decoration.

Background remains one detail level simpler than characters.

## 11. Global style

- European fantasy adventure ensemble comic illustration;
- clean stable black hand-drawn line;
- flat colors + restrained cel shading;
- bright but controlled saturation;
- approximately 5–5.5 heads tall;
- head slightly enlarged but not chibi;
- readable faces and hands;
- clear species silhouettes;
- no depth-of-field blur;
- no painterly/realistic/3D/cinematic concept-art drift.

## 12. Production order

1. official research;
2. subscene selection;
3. scene-state lock;
4. population model;
5. species scale;
6. faction/culture visual language;
7. scene-specific casting assets;
8. props and creatures;
9. event islands;
10. actor blocking;
11. final scene;
12. scene correction/QC;
13. hidden-object design;
14. level data and UI;
15. archive/current-state update.

Order is enforced by Gate 0–7.

## 13. Level package contract

Every completed level must eventually contain:
- final scene image;
- target set;
- answer locations;
- clue crops / target thumbnails;
- UI assets;
- level metadata;
- revision-capable production assets.

## 14. Failure handling

Do not invent a new methodology for a local problem.

Fix in this order:
1. proportion;
2. species anatomy;
3. face/expression;
4. event/action;
5. density;
6. blocking/composition;
7. background detail;
8. color;
9. hidden-object design;
10. UI/data.
